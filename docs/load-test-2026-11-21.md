# Load test and failover drill: dev, 21 November 2026

How the API behaves under load behind the AI gateway (ADR 0002), where it stops scaling,
and whether losing a model backend reaches users. All times UTC.

## Setup

- **Load:** Locust (`tests/load/locustfile.py`), simulated lawyers asking random answerable
  golden-set questions with 5–15 s between questions; each user is a different `X-Dev-User`,
  so the gateway limits and counts tokens per user. Report per stage: `tests/load/report.py`.
  A stage *breaks* above 1% errors or a p95 above 60 s.
- **dev:** API on Container Apps (0.5 vCPU, 1 GiB per replica, a new replica per 10
  concurrent requests, at most 5); API Management Developer as the AI gateway; gpt-4.1-mini
  at 150K tokens/min in East US 2 (primary) and France Central (failover).
- The AI Search semantic ranker was off (its free monthly quota had run out, PR #6), so
  retrieval was plain hybrid search.
- Run from a laptop in the Netherlands (the `load-test` workflow runs the same from GitHub
  once it is on main); an answer takes 7–30 s, so the network adds little.

## Ramp (run 1)

| users | answers | answers/min | p50 (s) | p95 (s) | p99 (s) | errors | replicas |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 13 | 7.1 | 7.4 | 14.9 | 28.1 | 0% | 1 |
| 5 | 37 | 19.9 | 7.2 | 9.6 | 13.9 | 0% | 1 |
| 10 | 95 | 31.8 | 7.6 | 18.3 | 21.1 | 0% | 1 |
| 20 | 83 | 29.1 | 29.0 | 53.5 | 55.0 | 0% | 1 → 2 |
| 30 | 77 | 28.5 | 54.0 | 86.8 | 88.1 | 0% | 2 → 3 |

**No errors at any stage. Throughput stops at about 30 answers a minute from 10 users on**,
and from then on extra users only wait longer: p95 passes 60 s at 30 users.

**The ceiling is model capacity, not the app.** An answer makes about 5 model calls
(routing, agent, tool calls, groundedness), about 15K tokens. 30 answers a minute is about
the 300K tokens/min of both regions together. The API's CPU stayed below 0.4 core on any
replica, and Container Apps scaled out to 3 replicas anyway, on concurrent requests that
were waiting for the model.

At the gateway during run 1:

| | calls |
|---|---|
| East US 2: answered / throttled (429) | 956 / 46 |
| France Central: answered / throttled | 551 / 39 |
| Gateway → app: 503 (both backends' circuit breakers open) | 511 |

The app's retries absorbed every 503, so users saw slower answers rather than errors.

## Circuit breaker tuning (run 2)

Run 1's breaker took a backend out for a minute after a single 429, so both regions were
often out at once while their capacity came back within seconds. Changed to 3 failures in
10 s, out for 10 s (or for Retry-After), and reran 20 and 30 users: 25–26 answers a minute,
p95 64 s and 100 s, no errors, 525 gateway 503s. **No measurable difference**: with both
regions full, when a breaker reopens doesn't matter. The rule is kept, as the more usual one.

## Failover drill: primary down

East US 2's chat deployment cut to 1K tokens/min (from 150K), then 3 users for 4 minutes.

- **First attempt (11:39):** no effect. All 199 calls still succeeded on East US 2: a
  capacity change is enforced only after a few minutes.
- **Second attempt:** the cut made at 11:44:31 was enforced at 11:50:47 (a probe got its
  first 429 after 376 s). Then the load:

| | calls |
|---|---|
| East US 2: answered / throttled | 9 / 22 |
| France Central: answered | 160 |
| Answers to users | 36, **0 errors**, p50 7.2 s, p95 37.5 s |

**Losing the primary didn't reach users:** the gateway moved the traffic to France
Central. The p95 rose from about 9 s to 37 s, because a call that hits the throttled primary
first waits for the retry. Capacity restored to 150K at 11:55.

## What this means

- To serve more than about 30 answers a minute, **raise model capacity**: Global Standard
  capacity costs nothing until used, and the quota allows far more (16M tokens/min for
  gpt-4.1-mini in East US 2). Fewer, cheaper calls per answer (routing without a model call,
  a smaller model for the groundedness check) would also help.
- More API replicas don't help: the API waits on the model. Scaling on concurrent requests
  still makes sense, as replicas are cheap and protect against slow requests piling up.
- The per-user limit (60K tokens/min) wasn't reached: the busiest simulated user used about
  27K a minute.
- For drills: wait until a capacity cut is enforced (probe for a 429) before you measure.

## Semantic cache (measured, then switched off)

The gateway's optional semantic cache (Azure Managed Redis, `SEMANTIC_CACHE=true`; partitioned
by a hash of the caller's access) was switched on for one run of
`tests/evaluation/evaluate_semantic_cache.py`: 20 answerable golden-set questions, asked
three times. Hits are the gateway's chat-completion calls that weren't forwarded to a model.

| pass | model calls at the gateway | from the cache | facts present | grounded | mean answer time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fill (first time) | 70 | 7% | 0.975 | 0.95 | 10.5 s |
| repeat (same questions, same user) | 71 | 18% | 0.95 | 0.95 | 10.8 s |
| other access (a paralegal on one matter) | 77 | 10% | 0.16 | 0.95 | 14.0 s |

- **Little to gain:** even an exact repeat came 82% from the model, and no repeated answer was
  word for word the same. Most model calls carry eight retrieved passages and the agent's
  tool steps, so they rarely match a stored prompt closely enough. Answer time didn't improve.
- **A real risk:** 7% of the calls in the first pass came from the cache although no question
  had been asked before: prompts that are *similar* were treated as the same. That's
  harmless for a routing decision and wrong for an answer.
- **Access separation held** by design: the paralegal's partition can't reach the lawyer's
  entries (its hits come from its own pass); its low fact score is what a paralegal may see.
- Quality otherwise held (one question lost a fact on the repeat).

**Kept off.** For this app it saves little, adds a cross-question risk, and costs about
€1–2 a day. It would pay off for short, repetitive prompts (FAQ-style questions without
retrieved passages); if it's used, cache only the classification calls, not the answers.
